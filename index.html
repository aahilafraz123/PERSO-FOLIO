The story, in plain words

Darkness to light.

I’m Aahil — a software engineer who learned the hard way that the only light is the one you carry. Here’s the honest arc, from no path at all to a seat in the arena.

01

The Wilderness

Three rounds of co-op applications. Zero interviews. A market that did not care how hard I was working or how much I wanted it. I watched peers land roles while I kept getting silence. No rejection — just nothing. Which is somehow worse.

What the wilderness teaches you that nothing else can is this: when nobody is pulling you forward you find out fast whether you pull yourself. I did not have a mentor mapping out my path or a network opening doors. I had time, frustration, and a choice about what to do with both. I chose to build anyway. Not because I knew it would work. Because standing still felt worse than moving in the dark.

“I chose to build anyway. Not because I knew it would work. Because standing still felt worse than moving in the dark.”
02

The Forge

My first role gave me almost nothing — no structure, no direction, no one checking if I was growing. Most people would have coasted. I treated it like a lab. I taught myself to ship things that actually worked, learned what it costs to stay in the work when nobody is watching, and started to understand the difference between being busy and building something real.

Then came LearnFlow. 30+ hour weeks on top of a full course load. A startup expo. A pitch competition. Faculty demos across the school. LearnFlow is an AI-powered learning platform — the kind of tool that actually understands how a student learns, not just what they are studying. It connects your notes, your exams, your assignments, and your study sessions into one intelligent experience that adapts to you over time. We grew to 150+ beta users — not from a launch moment but from listening, iterating, and going back to users with something meaningfully better than what they asked for.

LearnFlow did not just teach me to code. It taught me that I could figure out whatever I needed to figure out. That skill — learning how to learn — turned out to be worth more than anything on my transcript.

“That skill — learning how to learn — turned out to be worth more than anything on my transcript.”
03

The World Stage

With Team Relentless, we built something we believed in and entered it into one of the most competitive global hackathons in the world. Out of more than 11,500 projects submitted to the 2025 NASA Space Apps Challenge, ours was recognized as a Global Nominee — placing in the top 1,290 teams worldwide and earning acknowledgment from NASA for the work.

What made it mean something beyond the recognition was how we got there. One working weekend. An idea on Friday, a live production URL on Azure by Sunday — fully automated CI/CD pipeline, real data integrations, real users. We built an AI-powered air quality forecasting platform pulling from NASA TEMPO satellite data, OpenAQ ground sensors, and OpenWeather APIs, generating 6-hour AQI predictions and surfacing them through an interactive geospatial map and a conversational AI interface. From zero to production in 48 hours.

Most people never find out if what they build can stand next to the best in the world. We found out. It can. And we built it in a weekend.

“Most people never find out if what they build can stand next to the best in the world. We found out. It can.”
04

The Arena

Comcast is my first real corporate environment and I did not ease into it. Within my first week I identified and resolved a critical authentication vulnerability in a production system — nobody asked me to find it, I just found it. That set the tone for everything after.

My managers bet on me early and put me on something that mattered — a new org-wide AI initiative running a startup operating model inside a large enterprise. No traditional agile, no safety net, just build something real and make it count.

From there I co-built an AI-powered analytics engine that turns fragmented security data into clear, prioritized action for the people responsible for it. Instead of leaders drowning in dashboards and manually piecing together where the risk actually lives, the system does that work — ingesting data across sources, scoring it by what actually matters, and surfacing insights through a layer of specialized AI agents that understand the context of the organization they are analyzing. The agents do not just report data. They reason about it.

I also built two internal AI skills that changed how the engineering team operated day to day. Automated status updates across developer pods. Shared intelligence surfacing blockers, progress, and next steps without another meeting. Small tools. Real adoption. The kind of thing that disappears into the workflow and makes everything move faster.

The work reached senior leadership and was recognized at the organizational level as an example of what the initiative was built to produce. What that meant to me was not the recognition — it was knowing I had earned it starting from zero in an environment I had never operated in before.

I came into this co-op with something to prove. I leave having proved it — not to anyone else first, but to myself.

“I came into this co-op with something to prove. I leave having proved it — not to anyone else first, but to myself.”
“Money was the first motivator. Somewhere along the way, the work became the reason.”
