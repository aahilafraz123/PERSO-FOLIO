The Wilderness

Three rounds of co-op applications. Zero interviews. The market was not the problem — I was. My resume was outdated, my preparation was shallow, and the silence was the only feedback I needed.

The wilderness teaches you one thing: nothing gets handed to you. So I stopped waiting. Started feeding myself — projects, skills, reps — faster than I could process. Once I started, I could not stop.

An unpaid co-op with no structure taught me I needed to step up. So I built LearnFlow — an idea born from studying across five disconnected tools. First version in a weekend. The moment it worked, something shifted. I had made something real, from nothing. I wanted that feeling again. That was the first time the hunger showed up.

The World Stage

With Team Relentless, we built something we believed in and entered it into one of the most competitive global hackathons in the world. Out of more than 11,500 projects submitted to the 2025 NASA Space Apps Challenge, ours was recognized as a ↳ see NASA email — placing in the top 1,290 teams worldwide and earning acknowledgment from NASA for the work.

What made it mean something was how we got there. One working weekend. An idea on Friday, a live production URL on Azure by Sunday — fully automated CI/CD pipeline, real data integrations, real users. We built an AI-powered air quality forecasting platform pulling from NASA TEMPO satellite data, OpenAQ ground sensors, and OpenWeather APIs, generating 6-hour AQI predictions through an interactive geospatial map and a conversational AI interface. Zero to production in 48 hours.

Most people never find out if what they build can stand next to the best in the world. We found out. It can. And we built it in a weekend.

The Arena

Comcast is my first real corporate environment. My managers put me on a new org-wide AI initiative — a startup operating model inside a large enterprise. No traditional structure. Just build something real.

I co-built an AI-powered analytics engine that turns fragmented security data into clear, prioritized action. Not dashboards — reasoning. Specialized AI agents that understand organizational context and surface what actually matters. I also shipped two internal AI tools that changed how the engineering team operated: automated status updates, shared intelligence surfacing blockers without another meeting. Small tools. Real adoption.

The work reached senior leadership and was recognized for what it was. But the co-op is not over. I am still in it. Still building.
